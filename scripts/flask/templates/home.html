Mental Health Need Index

Models local mental health demand by proportionally allocating GP practice registers and prescribing data to LSOAs based on patient registration data.

Indicators: QOF Depression prevalence, QOF SMI prevalence, Antidepressant items per patient, and SAMHI index.

Need Index Map

Mental Health Access Index

Models primary healthcare accessibility combining clinical quality, physical travel times, rurality, vehicle access, and digital exclusion.

Indicators (12 total): SMI care plans (MH002), physical health reviews (MH021), PCA exception rates, depression reviews (DEP004), GP & Hospital travel times (Public Transport & Car), rural isolation, car availability, and DERI digital access.

Access Index Map

Access Gap Index

Calculates geographic disparities by comparing mental health need directly against healthcare accessibility (Need − Access).

Disparity Mapping: Divergent scale highlighting areas where clinical need outpaces accessibility.

Access Gap Index Map

Rural Risk Index

Rural vulnerability and isolation model combining geographic, transport, and socio-economic deprivation factors.

Indicators: Rural/Urban classification (2021), GP journey times (PT/Walk & Car), Car non-ownership (Census 2021), IMD 2025 deprivation rank, 2021/2 UK LSOA geodemographics (LSOAC), and Household composition (Census 2021 TS003).

Rural Risk Index Map

Small Area Mental Health Index (SAMHI)

Longitudinal analysis of the composite mental health index developed by the Place-Based Longitudinal Data Resource (PLDR).

12 Years of Data: Annual national indicators spanning 2011 through 2022.

Components: Mental health prescriptions, hospital admissions, and incapacity/disability benefits.

About the LUMHR Project

The Lincolnshire Unit for Mental Health Research (LUMHR) is a Centre of Excellence for applied, place-based mental health research focused on improving mental health outcomes in rural, coastal, and small urban communities across Lincolnshire. LUMHR works closely with local communities, NHS partners, and local authorities to better understand mental health needs, identify what works, and develop new approaches to support wellbeing.
